# Elvate Curriculum Expansion Plan

## Purpose

Extend the existing 22-class curriculum into a coherent path from beginner foundations toward independent, confident English use. This plan uses the live lesson content in `src/constants/curriculum.ts` as its source of truth. The curriculum summary in `PROJECT_REPORT_AND_ROADMAP.md` is stale in places, so use the lesson source rather than that summary when authoring content.

## Recommendation at a glance

- Keep the four current units and their 22 classes as the foundation.
- Add **eight units, Units 5–12, with five classes each**. This adds 40 classes and creates a 62-class pathway.
- Author and review one unit at a time. Start with Unit 5, check its flow with learners, then use that feedback before writing Unit 6.
- Keep each class compatible with the existing five-class manual course creator and the app's lesson model.

Eight more units provide room to close grammar gaps, improve listening and repair strategies, build workplace communication, and finish with transfer practice. More units are possible, but adding them before validating these eight risks repeating topics without making learners more capable.

## What the current four units already teach

The current curriculum has 22 classes and one consistent lesson cycle: Tamil mental model and English rule, wrong/right contrast, sentence jumble, guided translation, then spoken practice.

## Unit 5 — detailed authoring blueprint

**Unit title:** Everyday Grammar That Connects  
**Target:** A2 consolidation and a bridge toward B1  
**Unit outcome:** Learners can ask clear questions, describe what they do and what is happening, talk about past experience with the right time reference, and give simple details about quantity, place, and time.

| Class | Focus | Learner can… | Tamil-speaker trap to address |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Ask Clear Questions and Give Short Answers** | `do/does/did`, `be`, modal and `wh-` question patterns | Ask about routines, current situations, and past events; answer briefly and naturally. | “Where you went?” / “You are coming?” Teach the helper verb and word order, not a list of question words alone. |
| **2. Talk About Things and Amounts** | `a/an/the`, count and non-count nouns, `some/any`, `much/many/a few/a little` | Ask for, offer, and describe common items and amounts. | “an advice,” “many money,” and leaving out an article when English needs one. Use food, shopping, and workplace examples. |
| **3. Say What Usually Happens and What Is Happening Now** | present simple vs present continuous; common stative verbs | Contrast habits, schedules, temporary actions, and states. | “I am knowing,” “I am understanding,” or using one present form for every meaning. Recycle the Unit 1 routine context with new contrasts. |
| **4. Connect Past Events to Experience** | past simple vs present perfect; finished time vs life experience | Say when something happened and describe experience when the exact time is not the point. | “I have seen him yesterday.” Make the time cue visible and practice both versions side by side. |
| **5. Be Precise About Place and Time** | `in/on/at`, `to/from/for`, high-frequency verb + preposition pairs | Explain where, when, and where-to; make and respond to a simple plan. | Directly translating Tamil postpositions into English. Recycle appointments, directions, calls, and travel from Units 1–3. |

**Unit 5 review check:** Give the learner a short scenario that combines a question, one present-time contrast, one quantity or preposition, and a past-experience response. The scenario should require new sentences, not memorized answers from the lesson.

## Units 6–12 — five-class outlines

### Unit 6: Tell Clear Stories

**Target outcome:** Tell a short personal or work story with a clear beginning, sequence, and ending; respond to a listener's follow-up.

1. Put events in order with `first`, `then`, `after that`, and `finally`.
2. Add background with past continuous and choose it against past simple.
3. Describe experiences with present perfect and add a specific past detail.
4. Explain a problem and its result using cause-and-effect connectors.
5. Tell a 60–90 second story and answer two natural follow-up questions.

### Unit 7: Listen, Clarify & Recover

**Target outcome:** Stay in a conversation when speech is fast, a detail is unclear, or a word is unfamiliar.

1. Listen for the topic and the speaker's main point.
2. Ask for repetition, slower speech, or spelling politely.
3. Confirm a name, date, number, instruction, or next step.
4. Check understanding and correct a misunderstanding without blame.
5. Complete a phone or service conversation using clarification and confirmation.

Use short, natural spoken prompts with varied pacing. The goal is comprehension and repair, not merely repeating a transcript.

### Unit 8: Confident Team Communication

**Target outcome:** Contribute clearly to a team conversation and leave others knowing the update, blocker, request, and next action.

1. Give a concise progress update: done, in progress, next.
2. Explain a blocker and ask for the specific help needed.
3. Make and respond to a polite request with a clear deadline or condition.
4. Summarize a decision and confirm action items after a meeting.
5. Write a short work message and say the same message naturally aloud.

This should extend the phone, delay, and meeting lessons already present rather than repeat their role-play scripts.

### Unit 9: Grammar for Meaning and Precision

**Target outcome:** Use more than one sentence pattern to explain conditions, describe people or things, and report what someone said.

1. Use real and likely conditionals for plans and consequences.
2. Use `would` / `could` for hypothetical choices and polite possibilities.
3. Join ideas with relative clauses (`who`, `which`, `that`).
4. Report a message or request accurately; contrast direct and reported speech.
5. Choose active or passive voice based on whether the actor or result matters.

Introduce one main grammar decision per class. Avoid presenting a grammar label without showing what meaning changes.

### Unit 10: Understand and Explain Information

**Target outcome:** Extract the important information from a short spoken or written message and explain it accurately in the learner's own words.

1. Find the main point and separate it from examples.
2. Catch names, dates, numbers, and constraints in an announcement or message.
3. Separate a speaker's opinion from supporting facts and reasons.
4. Compare two options and give a short, organized summary.
5. Explain a short update, instruction, or data point and answer a follow-up.

Keep inputs short enough for the current class format. Build difficulty by increasing natural phrasing and reducing hints, not by making source texts unnecessarily long.

### Unit 11: Persuade, Negotiate & Lead

**Target outcome:** Make a clear proposal, support it with reasons, listen to a different view, and reach or explain a decision.

1. State a position and give a relevant reason or example.
2. Agree partly, concede a point, and explain a different conclusion.
3. Make a proposal and respond to a concern or objection.
4. Negotiate a deadline, priority, or compromise politely.
5. Give a short persuasive pitch and handle two unscripted questions.

Build on the debate and elevator-pitch introduction in Unit 4 by adding evidence, objections, and listener questions.

### Unit 12: Independent English Capstone

**Target outcome:** Transfer the learner's skills to unfamiliar situations, maintain a longer conversation, and recognize what to practice next.

1. Handle an unexpected travel, appointment, or service problem.
2. Explain a personal or work achievement with context and result.
3. Join a meeting discussion, ask a clarifying question, and summarize a decision.
4. Deliver a two-minute talk with a clear opening, structure, and close.
5. Complete a mixed-situation speaking challenge, reflect on recurring errors, and make a personal practice plan.

Unit 4 already contains an elevator-pitch capstone. Unit 12 is broader: it tests transfer across situations and combines listening, clarification, explanation, and follow-up.

## Keep the existing class flow, deepen the learning

Every new class should fit the current app's lesson structure:

1. **Tamil mental model and rule:** Explain the intended English meaning in conversational Tamil; show a concise English pattern and natural example.
2. **Wrong/right contrast:** Use one realistic Tamil-to-English transfer error and explain why the natural form works.
3. **Jumble:** Use a grammatical, useful sentence that directly practices the target pattern. Keep punctuation attached to the right word.
4. **Guided translation:** Provide a natural Tamil prompt and three or four English choices. Distractors should each represent a plausible learner error; only one answer is correct.
5. **Speaking:** Give a situation and a clear communicative goal. Include a model answer and alternatives, but let the learner make it personal.

The app currently presents these as lecture, jumble, translation, and spoken practice. Keep authored material complete for every step; do not treat the spoken step as an optional add-on.

## Authoring and quality checklist

Before publishing a class, check all of the following:

- It teaches **one primary decision** and builds on something already introduced.
- The example is natural, useful, and appropriate to the learner's target level.
- The Tamil explanation describes the thinking difference; it is not a word-for-word translation of the English rule.
- The wrong example is a real learner trap, not a silly sentence that is too easy to reject.
- The jumble has one unambiguous natural answer and `scrambledWords` is an exact permutation of `correctWords`.
- Every translation option is grammatical enough to be plausible, but exactly one is correct for the prompt.
- The speaking task requires the learner to communicate an intent, not just repeat the target sentence.
- Names, places, dates, prices, answer keys, and grammar have been reviewed by a fluent English reviewer.
- A class is not considered “advanced” just because it contains rare vocabulary or a long explanation.

## Suggested release sequence

1. Draft Unit 5 and test it with beginner/intermediate Tamil speakers.
2. Note where they hesitate, select distractors, or cannot make a personal spoken answer; revise those classes before adding the next unit.
3. Add Units 6–8 to build storytelling, listening repair, and collaboration.
4. Add Units 9–11 for precision, information handling, and persuasion.
5. Release Unit 12 only after the earlier classes provide the vocabulary and speaking patterns it expects.

This path aims for broad, usable fluency. No finite lesson list can teach “everything” in English; sustained listening, real conversation, reading, and feedback remain necessary for long-term mastery.
